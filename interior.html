True, and made up

What the sources say, and what this sample invents

  • From the record. Abraham Gesner opened his museum in Saint John in 1842. The Natural History Society of New Brunswick (founded 1862) took over that collection in 1890 and moved its museum to a building on Union Street in 1906. These collections later became the New Brunswick Museum.
  • From the record. The Fern Ledges plants, collected by C. F. Hartt, were the first specimens the Society bought. The insect wings found there were once called the oldest insects known, and were later re-dated.
  • From the record. Gesner’s specimen No. 906 carries a label reading “From the Collection of Mohs”.
  • Invented. No source I found describes the rooms inside the Union Street building, so the floor plan, wallpaper, furniture and the attendant are made up to suit the period.
  • Left out for now. The Society also held Wolastoqey and Mi’kmaq belongings. How the game shows them is a decision to make with care, ideally with the communities themselves.
  • Sources. New Brunswick Museum (Wikipedia); Gesner’s Museum of Natural History (Geoscience Canada); George Frederic Matthew (Canadian Encyclopedia); Stonehammer Geopark.
