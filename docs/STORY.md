# The story: the opening and chapter 1

The words are all in `js/story.js`. This file is the plan behind them: what each part is for, and the rules for
pacing work with story. (The desk's own plan is in `docs/ONBOARDING.md`.)

## What the opening has to do

The first version opened with seven narrator lines about the Natural History Society: 1862, Gesner, 1890, 1906. The
user's verdict: you lose the kids right there. The opening now does three jobs instead, and teaches the Society's
history later, on exhibit cards and in Mr. Harrow's grumbling:

1. **Who you are.** The archivist is a compulsive cataloguer who labels everything, their sandwich included. The joke is
   also the game's skill: who made it, and when.
2. **The theme, as a joke, not a lesson.** Percy Gage sells comet pills on the train. Your one choice is to ask
   *"Which papers? Who says it's poison?"* He can't say. That is sourcing, and nobody calls it that.
3. **A question to pull the player on.** The job letter's P.S. ("do not touch the boxes on the bottom shelf"), Mr.
   Tull who "left suddenly", and later his card in your stuck drawer: Box 17 is missing.

## The places

- **Union Street, No. 72**: the museum (already built).
- **King Square, Sydney Street and the old burial ground** (area `square`): the hub between home and work. Real: the
  square (1785), its Union Jack paths (1840s), the 1908 bandstand, the Loyalist Burial Ground beside it (1783, oldest
  stone 1784, which runs to Union Street). Three catalogue cards out here.
- **Mrs. Hanlon's boarding house** (area `lodgings`, invented): her kitchen and your room, with Mr. Tull's locked trunk.
- **The train from outside** (area `line`): the opening only.
- Next, when it earns its place: **the City Market** (1876), signposted from King Square ("CHARLOTTE ST. · MARKET") but
  shut for now; the newspaper office for chapter 2; Miss Lyle's school.

Every place has a job in the story. Scenery alone is not a reason to build one.

## Pacing work and story

The rhythm for every day: **story beat → go somewhere → desk work → the world changes → a hook.**

- At most about **six lines of dialogue** before the player does something (walks, chooses, opens something).
- **One desk case at a time** (5–8 tasks, about ten minutes), then back out into the world.
- **Work changes the world**: a letter comes back, a visitor arrives, a place opens, a clue turns up.
- **Every day ends on a question.**

Chapter 1 now has three desk sessions on day 2, each followed by a walk to the stacks and a talk with Mr. Harrow, and
the comet night after them. The practice paper is the only desk work on the first evening.

## The mystery (kept small for now)

Mr. Tull, the archivist before you, left in April without a word and never came back for his trunk. His card: *Box 17
is not on the bottom shelf. Somebody has been in the stacks at night. Say nothing to H. until I am sure.* Mr. Harrow's
letter asks you not to touch the bottom shelf. On the comet night you decide whether to show him the card.

Rules: human and grounded (missing papers, a person who left in a hurry), never supernatural; each chapter turns up one
piece, proven with evidence; the answer is the user's to decide. Nothing in the game yet says what Box 17 held.

## What's invented and what's real

Invented: every named person except the authors of the sources and the people named on the newspaper's front page;
Mrs. Hanlon's house; the crowd's words; the cabman. Real: the places, the dates, the newspaper, the comet and the
pills, the foghorn, the tides, the Reversing Falls, the King's death. Sources: `docs/SOURCES.md`.
